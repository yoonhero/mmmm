\section{Furstenberg's proof of the infinitude of primes}

Let a basis with 

\[
    a+n\Z = \set{a+kn: k\in \Z}
\]

for $a \in \Z, n \in \N$

First, every basis also be closed. Since

\[
    \Z \setminus (a+n\Z) = \bigcup_{r \not\equiv a} (r+n\Z)
\]

Second, non-empty finite set cannot be open. Because $\exists b$ in basis s.t. $b \subset F$.

Suppose there's finite primes $p_1, p_2, ..., p_2$.

\[
    \Z \setminus \set{-1, 1} = p_1\Z \cup p_2\Z \cup ... \cup p_3\Z
\]

is closed then $\set{-1, 1}$ be open. Then contradiction.
